BEV Regular-Driving Audit Recommendations
=========================================

Summary
-------
The current regular-driving BEV failure mode does not look like a major observation-construction bug.
The BEV agents already receive the same privileged 8D state vector as the non-vision agents, plus the
84x84x1 BEV image. The more likely cause of the "hard steering into wall" shortcut is the combination
of weak forward terminal penalty, aggressive action noise, and lower sample efficiency of the vision policy.

Key Findings
------------
1. The BEV agent is not image-only.
   - It receives the 8D vector:
     [steering, hitch angle, tractor CTE, tractor heading error,
      trailer CTE, trailer heading error, curvature_10, curvature_20]
   - It also receives the BEV image.
   - Therefore the problem is unlikely to be simple information starvation.

2. The forward dense reward makes early crash policies too competitive.
   - Non-success termination penalty is only -10.
   - Nonterminal progress reward is 0.5 * xd.
   - With fixed speed near 5 m/s, that gives about +2.5 reward per step before penalties.
   - A weak early policy can collect several positive-reward steps, then crash, and still not be punished strongly enough.

3. Exploration noise is large relative to the steering bound.
   - action_noise_sigma = 0.3
   - steering action bound is about +/-0.436 rad/s
   - This means the noise standard deviation is roughly 69 percent of the full steering limit.
   - For a 1D fixed-speed steering policy, this is aggressive and can repeatedly drive the policy into saturated steering behavior.

4. The BEV feature extractor is more sample-hungry than the state/lidar MLP policies.
   - This is expected and does not by itself mean the BEV observation is "too large."
   - The image branch is valid, but it will usually require more data and cleaner reward/exploration settings.

5. The BEV image pipeline appears sane.
   - Image shape, dtype, and value range were normal in quick runtime checks.
   - Constant zero steer survived.
   - Constant max steer left/right crashed quickly in both BEV and state envs.
   - That points away from a malformed image pipeline and toward learning dynamics.

Interpretation
--------------
The current BEV collapse is best explained as a reward/exploration/sample-efficiency problem, not mainly
an observation-size problem and not mainly a missing-information problem.

Recommendations
---------------
1. Do not rely on increasing training timesteps alone.
   - More timesteps may help, but only after reward and exploration are adjusted.
   - Otherwise the agent may simply practice the bad shortcut for longer.

2. Reduce action noise for regular driving.
   - Suggested starting values:
     - 0.1
     - or 0.05
   - This is especially important for BEV forward lane-following, where the policy learns more slowly.

3. Increase the forward terminal failure penalty.
   - The current -10 penalty is likely too weak.
   - Crashing into the wall should be made clearly worse than short-term progress accumulation.

4. Prefer guided reward for BEV regular driving.
   - Use the guided curriculum for the simple lane-following case until the policy reliably avoids collapse.
   - This should help the vision policy discover a stable steering strategy before learning purely from dense reward.

5. Increase BEV training only after the above changes.
   - Suggested range:
     - 400k timesteps
     - 800k timesteps
   - This is more justified after reducing exploration noise and strengthening the failure penalty.

6. Keep obstacle work separate for now.
   - Focus only on regular driving until BEV lane-following is stable.
   - Do not use obstacle results to judge the BEV regular-driving setup yet.

Bottom Line
-----------
The current evidence suggests:
- not primarily an information-deficit problem
- not primarily that the BEV observation is too large
- primarily a reward plus exploration plus sample-efficiency problem for the vision policy
