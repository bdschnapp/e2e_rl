The articulated system adds a trailer body coupled to the tractor through a revolute hitch.
The relevant geometry is:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $L_h$: signed distance from the tractor rear axle to the hitch point (in both the
    training model and the hardware platform the hitch sits at the rear axle, so
    $L_h \approx 0$);
    \item $L_t$: distance from the hitch to the trailer axle, i.e.\ the trailer wheelbase;
    \item $\gamma = \psi - \psi_t$: the articulation angle between the tractor heading $\psi$
    and the trailer heading $\psi_t$ (the same convention used by the deployment in
    \cref{ch:sim_to_real}).
\end{itemize}

The hitch point is carried rigidly by the tractor,
\begin{align}
    x_h &= x - L_h\cos\psi, & y_h &= y - L_h\sin\psi,
    \label{eq:hitch_point}
\end{align}
and translates with the tractor's longitudinal speed $v$. Imposing a pure-rolling (no lateral
slip) constraint on the trailer axle fixes the trailer yaw rate from the lateral component of
the hitch velocity:

\begin{equation}
    \dot{\psi}_t = \frac{v}{L_t}\sin(\psi - \psi_t) = \frac{v}{L_t}\sin\gamma .
    \label{eq:trailer_yaw}
\end{equation}

The articulation angle therefore evolves as

\begin{equation}
    \dot{\gamma} = \dot{\psi} - \frac{v}{L_t}\sin\gamma ,
    \label{eq:hitch_kinematics}
\end{equation}

where the tractor yaw rate $\dot{\psi}$ is supplied by the tractor model of
\cref{eq:tractor_ss} (equivalently $\dot{\psi} = (v/L)\tan\delta$ for a purely kinematic
tractor of wheelbase $L = l_f + l_r$). The trailer axle pose then follows from the hitch point
and the trailer heading,
\begin{align}
    x_t &= x_h - L_t\cos\psi_t, & y_t &= y_h - L_t\sin\psi_t .
    \label{eq:trailer_axle}
\end{align}

The sign of $v$ in \cref{eq:hitch_kinematics} is what makes reverse motion qualitatively
harder. Linearizing about the aligned configuration ($\gamma \approx 0$, so
$\sin\gamma \approx \gamma$) and holding the steering-driven tractor yaw fixed, the unforced
articulation mode obeys

\begin{equation}
    \dot{\gamma} \approx -\frac{v}{L_t}\,\gamma,
    \qquad \lambda = -\frac{v}{L_t} .
    \label{eq:hitch_eig}
\end{equation}

In forward motion ($v > 0$) the eigenvalue $\lambda$ is negative. Articulation errors decay
and the trailer self-aligns behind the tractor. In reverse ($v < 0$) the eigenvalue becomes
positive and the same errors grow exponentially toward the jackknife condition. This is an
open-loop instability rather than a mere tuning difficulty, and it is the defining feature of
reverse articulated manoeuvring, the reason the reverse task demands active hitch
stabilization, trailer-referenced error metrics, and the dedicated reward and curriculum
treatments developed in \cref{ch:learning_framework}.
